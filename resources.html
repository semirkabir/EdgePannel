The field guide.
Terms, methods, no mystique.

The vocabulary open-source intelligence work actually uses, and the full methodology behind our instability index — including where it can be wrong. An informed reader is the point, not the risk.

Glossary

The language of the map.

Every term below appears somewhere in the dashboard. If you meet a word in the app that isn't here, that's a bug — tell us on GitHub.

OSINT
Open-Source Intelligence — analysis built exclusively from publicly available information: news, official releases, transponder data, satellite imagery, public registries.
ADS-B
The transponder broadcast most aircraft transmit — position, altitude, callsign. What powers the flight and military-air layers.
AIS
The maritime equivalent of ADS-B: ships broadcasting identity, position and heading. Vessels that go "dark" (stop transmitting) are themselves a signal.
GEOINT
Geospatial intelligence — anything derived from imagery and location data: satellite photos, nighttime lights, fire detections.
SIGINT
Signals intelligence — intercepted communications and emissions. EdgePannel carries only its public shadow: jamming reports, GPS interference zones, outage maps.
Chokepoint
A narrow passage global trade can't route around — Hormuz, Suez, Malacca, Bab el-Mandeb. Small disruptions there move global prices.
CII
Country Instability Index — our 0–100 composite score per country. Full methodology below.
Escalation signal
An event that historically precedes conflict intensification: force posture changes, evacuation orders, airspace closures, sanctioned-asset movement.
Dark vessel
A ship that has switched off its AIS transponder — routine for some navies, a red flag near sanctions routes.
NOTAM
Notice to Air Missions — official alerts about airspace restrictions. Sudden military NOTAMs over a region are a classic early indicator.
Feed clustering
Grouping many articles about one event into a single story, so 40 headlines about the same strike read as one signal, not 40.
Source tiering
Ranking sources by authority — wire services and official channels above aggregators and blogs — so the loudest source isn't mistaken for the most reliable.
State-affiliated media
Outlets controlled by or aligned with a government. Carried and labeled — what a state chooses to say is itself intelligence.
GDACS
The UN/EC Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System — authoritative multi-hazard alerts for quakes, cyclones, floods and volcanoes.
Kp index
A 0–9 measure of geomagnetic storm strength. High Kp disrupts GPS, HF radio and satellites — infrastructure risk from space.
BGP / route leak
The internet's routing protocol, and the class of accident (or attack) where traffic detours through the wrong network. Visible in the outage layers.

Methodology

How the instability index is scored.

The CII is a 0–100 composite recomputed every few minutes server-side and cached for ten. This page explains what the score means; the exact weights, caps and floors live in the scoring documentation, which is kept in step with the code that computes them.

1 · A structural baseline (40%)

Each tier-1 country carries a static baseline reflecting structural risk — an active-war country starts high, a stable democracy starts low. The baseline anchors the score so a quiet news day doesn't make a war zone look like Switzerland.

2 · Live events (60%)

The dynamic part blends five weighted components from geocoded events in roughly the last 24–48 hours:

  • Conflict — battles, explosions, strikes, fatalities
  • Unrest — protests, riots, major outages
  • Information — news-signal pressure
  • Economic — economic stress signals
  • Security — military activity, GPS interference

Conflict carries the most weight, security the least. The exact split is in the scoring documentation.

3 · Environmental boosts

Capped additive pressure from ongoing hazards — severe climate events, cyber activity, shipping disruption and large wildfires each add a bounded number of points, so a hurricane can't masquerade as a civil war. The per-hazard caps are listed in the scoring documentation.

4 · The conflict floor

Countries in an active interstate or civil war (per UCDP classification) never score below 70; minor armed conflict floors at 50. Whatever the day's event count, the score cannot pretend a war away.

Honest limitations

The index reads what has happened; it does not forecast. Coverage bias is real — countries with dense reporting generate more events than closed ones, which is why the structural baseline exists. Treat the trend and the components as the signal, and the single number as the summary.
